# CM4DI Journal V2 — Evidence-Backed Novelty Positioning

## Positioning principle
Do not claim that CM4DI is the first digital-identity ontology. The literature now contains at least one recent peer-reviewed UFO-grounded SSI ontology (MFSSIA) and multiple ontology-oriented surveys, IAM ontology/metamodels, formal SSI models and reference frameworks.

## Defensible contribution hypothesis for the journal extension
CM4DI's contribution should be demonstrated as a **UFO-grounded cross-paradigm reference ontology for semantic interoperability across heterogeneous digital-identity ecosystems**, rather than as an ontology for one protocol, architecture or identity-management paradigm.

The differentiating combination to test and demonstrate is:
1. **Cross-paradigm scope** — centralized/fragmented, federation/SSO, enterprise IAM, wallet/VC/SSI, government trust ecosystems and non-human/workload/device/agent identity.
2. **Foundational grounding** — UFO/OntoUML analysis of identity-bearing entities, representations, roles, events, evidence, credentials, trust and lifecycle semantics.
3. **Core + Profiles architecture** — a minimal protocol/vendor-neutral Core with explicit Enterprise/Federation, Wallet/VC, Machine/Workload/Device/Agent and Trust/Assurance/Government profiles.
4. **Semantic anti-conflation** — systematic treatment of collisions such as Subject vs Account/Profile, Verifier meanings, IAM Role vs UFO Role, Credential vs Token, IdentitySource vs IdP, TrustFramework vs TrustDomain, and Device vs Authenticator.
5. **Traceable cross-ecosystem mappings** — standards, government frameworks, cloud/IAM products and reference implementations mapped to neutral concepts rather than copied into Core.
6. **Executable formalization** — OWL plus SHACL, stable identifiers, versioning and machine-readable mappings.
7. **Triangulated evaluation** — competency questions/SPARQL, logical validation, standards/platform coverage, conformance/reference implementations and empirical dataset instantiation.
8. **Reproducibility** — evidence registry, provenance, immutable releases and citable artifacts.

## Closest scholarly threats to novelty
### MFSSIA (Kormiltsyn et al., 2026)
Strongest direct ontology competitor because it is recent, peer-reviewed and UFO-grounded. CM4DI must differentiate by scope and interoperability mission, not by foundational-ontology use alone. MFSSIA is focused on blockchain-based multi-factor challenge-set SSI authentication in M2X.

### Comb & Martin (2026)
Strongest broad conceptual precursor. It surveys thousands of digital-identity publications and explicitly produces ontological fragments toward future formalization. CM4DI must acknowledge this work and position itself as the formal engineering/evaluation step beyond preliminary fragments, while avoiding the claim that broad ontology-oriented synthesis is novel by itself.

### Richter & Anke (2026)
Strongest recent SSI conceptual framework. It highlights governance and warns against conflating SSI principles with technical artifacts. CM4DI should adopt this lesson by keeping SSI principles/governance separate from technical Core constructs.

### Yildiz et al. (2023)
Strong SSI interoperability reference model. CM4DI must demonstrate that its interoperability contribution is semantic and cross-paradigm rather than another layered SSI architecture.

### Nahar (2021)
Strong enterprise IAM ontology/metamodel precursor. CM4DI must distinguish reference-ontology semantics from enterprise-architecture metamodeling.

## Claims to avoid
- “The first ontology of digital identity.”
- “The first UFO-grounded identity ontology.”
- “The first interoperable identity model.”
- “A universal ontology that covers every identity domain.”
- “Validation by a dataset proves ontology correctness.”

## Safer paper-level novelty language
A defensible formulation is:
> Existing work provides broad surveys and ontological fragments, SSI-specific conceptual/reference models, enterprise IAM metamodels, and a recent UFO-grounded ontology for a specialized SSI authentication setting. CM4DI addresses a different gap: a foundationally grounded, modular reference ontology designed to align heterogeneous identity paradigms, standards, trust frameworks and operational IAM ecosystems through explicit semantic mappings and reproducible multi-layer evaluation.

This wording remains provisional until Issue #9 completes cross-stream normalization and Gate B fixes the final scope.
