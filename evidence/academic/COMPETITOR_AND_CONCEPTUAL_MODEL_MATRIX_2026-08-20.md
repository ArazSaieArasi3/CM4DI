## High-confidence scholarly convergence
Across the retained literature, the following themes recur and are independently reinforced by the normative/operational streams:
1. Identity must be separated from identifiers, accounts/profiles and credentials.
2. Credential exchange roles are contextual roles; the same actor can occupy issuer/holder/verifier roles in different situations.
3. Authentication, authorization and identification are distinct processes.
4. Governance/trust cannot be reduced to cryptographic mechanisms.
5. Interoperability failures are semantic as well as protocol/implementation problems.
6. Identity lifecycle and credential lifecycle should not be collapsed.
7. SSI/wallet technology is one paradigm, not a synonym for digital identity.
8. Context, provenance, status and trust conditions are first-class concerns for interoperability.

## Closest-competitor conclusion
MFSSIA is the strongest direct ontology competitor because it is recent, peer-reviewed and UFO-grounded. Comb & Martin 2026 is the strongest broad scholarly precursor because it explicitly argues for ontological development across fragmented digital-identity research. Yildiz et al. is the strongest SSI interoperability reference-model comparator, while Nahar is the strongest enterprise-IAM ontology/metamodel comparator in the retained set.

CM4DI should therefore avoid weak novelty claims such as “the first ontology of digital identity.” The defensible contribution should instead be demonstrated through the combination of cross-paradigm scope, UFO/OntoUML grounding, Core/Profile separation, explicit standard/government/IAM/workload mappings, machine-readable OWL/SHACL artifacts, competency-question evaluation and reproducible multi-evidence validation.
