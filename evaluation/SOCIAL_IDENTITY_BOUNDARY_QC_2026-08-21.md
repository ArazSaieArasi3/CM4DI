## Validated findings
1. **Digital identity and social/online identity are adjacent but non-equivalent research objects.** Recent transdisciplinary work explicitly distinguishes system mechanisms from online social selfhood.
2. **Social Digital Identity Theory strengthens the case for a separate lane.** Group membership, salience, online/offline identity and platform affordances require psychological/social semantics that should not be embedded in IAM Core.
3. **Social login is not social identity.** It is an authentication/federation pattern using an external identity provider and remains within CM4DI Enterprise/Federation mappings.
4. **Role semantics require strict separation.** Role Identity, UFO SocialRole, IAM product roles and protocol interaction roles are different constructs.
5. **Membership and affiliation are bridgeable without Core duplication.** W3C ORG provides reusable organizational membership/role semantics; social facts can also appear as Claim/Attribute content.
6. **Persona/Profile and Reputation/Trust are high-risk lexical collisions.** A curated persona is not a technical account/profile record, and social reputation is not identical to trust or assurance.
7. **IdentityContext is narrower than social context.** Audience, salience and context collapse require a richer social-context theory and should stay outside Core.
8. **A future independent ontology is justified.** The retained construct family is coherent and sufficiently distinct to merit a UFO-grounded Social Identity Ontology research program connected to CM4DI through bridges.

## Residual limitations
1. This bounded analysis does not exhaust identity scholarship across sociology, psychology, anthropology, political science, cultural studies and HCI.
2. A future Social Identity Ontology would require dedicated research questions, review protocol and ethical treatment of socially sensitive classifications and attributed identities.
3. W3C ORG and SDHSS Social Life Core are useful bridges/benchmarks but do not themselves provide a complete theory of psychosocial identity.
4. `Social-Future` is a research-governance disposition, not an ontological category.
5. No social-identity class should be admitted to CM4DI merely because a platform stores social attributes or affiliations.
